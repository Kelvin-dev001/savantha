principles:
  label: Screening
  heading: What makes finance Shariah-compliant?
  lead: >-
    A product is Shariah-compliant when its structure, not only its name, meets five
    principles. These are the questions we ask of every option before we put it in front of
    you.
  items:
    - term: Riba
      gloss: No interest
      detail: >-
        Money may not earn money by itself. Returns must come from trade, leasing or a shared
        venture, never from a fixed charge on a loan.
    - term: Gharar
      gloss: No excessive uncertainty
      detail: >-
        Both sides must know what is being exchanged, at what price and on what terms. Hidden
        conditions and vague contracts fail the test.
    - term: Maysir
      gloss: No speculation
      detail: >-
        No gambling, and no arrangements where one side's gain depends on chance rather than
        effort, trade or a real asset.
    - term: Asset-backed
      gloss: Tied to something real
      detail: >-
        Every transaction is linked to a tangible asset or service: a house, stock, equipment,
        a business activity you can point to.
    - term: Ethical sectors
      gloss: Clean activity
      detail: >-
        No alcohol, gambling, tobacco, conventional interest-based finance or other activity
        the principles prohibit, directly or through a partner.

faqs:
  - q: How is Shariah-compliant finance different from a normal loan?
    a: >-
      A conventional loan charges interest on money lent. A Shariah-compliant structure does
      not. The financier buys, leases or shares in a real asset or venture, and earns from that
      trade or partnership instead. The total cost can be similar, so we compare both on the
      same terms before you decide.
  - q: Is Shariah-compliant finance only for Muslims?
    a: >-
      No. Anyone can use it, and many non-Muslim clients choose it because it is tied to real
      assets and avoids speculation and certain sectors. The principles are the same for
      everyone. We explain them plainly and help you judge whether a particular structure suits
      your goals and your appetite for risk.
  - q: Can I buy a home on Shariah-compliant terms in Kenya?
    a: >-
      Yes. Several Kenyan banks offer home finance on Shariah terms, usually through murabaha
      (cost-plus sale) or ijara (lease to own). We explain how each works, what you pay in
      total, what you own and when, and what happens if you miss a payment, so you can compare
      them properly.
  - q: Who checks that an option is really compliant?
    a: >-
      Each product is approved by its provider's own Shariah board. We read the structure, test
      it against the five principles and flag anything that needs a closer look. Our reviewer
      oversees that work. For a personal ruling, we encourage you to consult your own scholar,
      and we can prepare a note for them.
  - q: Do you recommend specific Islamic banks or funds?
    a: >-
      No. We are not licensed by the Capital Markets Authority and we do not recommend specific
      products or securities. We explain the structures available, compare their terms, costs
      and risks, and help you prepare the right questions. The choice of provider stays
      entirely yours.
  - q: Can you help us plan an estate under Islamic inheritance rules?
    a: >-
      Yes, alongside your advocate. We help you understand how faraid allocates shares, what
      can be left by will within the permitted third, and how property, savings and business
      interests are currently held. Your advocate drafts the documents; we make sure the
      financial picture behind them is clear.
