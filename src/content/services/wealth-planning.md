faqs:
  - q: What is wealth planning consultancy?
    a: >-
      It is a structured review of your whole financial picture, followed by a written plan.
      We look at income, spending, debts, cover, savings and property together, put your goals
      in order, and explain the cost and risk of each option. You make the decisions; we make
      sure they are informed ones.
  - q: Do I need to be wealthy to work with you?
    a: >-
      No. The name puts some people off, but most of our planning starts with ordinary
      questions: where the salary goes, how to stop borrowing before payday, how much to keep
      in reserve. A plan matters most before there is much wealth to plan, because the early
      habits decide what comes later.
  - q: Will you tell me which investments to buy?
    a: >-
      No. Savantho is not licensed by the Capital Markets Authority, so we do not recommend
      specific securities or funds. We help you understand the types of options available,
      what they cost and what could go wrong. Where you need regulated investment advice, we
      refer you to a CMA-licensed adviser.
  - q: What will you need from me?
    a: >-
      Recent payslips or business income figures, bank and mobile money statements, loan
      statements, insurance policies, pension statements and details of any property. We send
      a short checklist after the first call. Everything you share is kept confidential and
      used only to prepare your plan.
  - q: How are your fees set?
    a: >-
      We quote a fixed fee for the plan in our written proposal, before any work starts. Reviews
      are priced separately and agreed in advance. You will know the full cost before you
      commit, and we explain any fee charged by anyone we introduce you to.
  - q: Can you help us plan for our children's education?
    a: >-
      Yes. Education is one of the goals we cost and date most often. We work out what each
      stage is likely to cost, how much to set aside and when, and how that fits alongside
      your reserve, your home and your retirement, so one goal does not quietly crowd out
      another.
