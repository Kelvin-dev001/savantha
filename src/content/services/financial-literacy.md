  Financial literacy training gives people the habits and confidence to manage money well. We

format: Workshops and session series, on site or online, in English or Kiswahili
audience:
  - Employers who see staff under pressure from debt and payday borrowing.
  - SACCOs that want members to borrow, save and plan with more confidence.
  - NGOs and programmes that need a financial capability strand that works.
  - Schools, colleges and youth groups preparing people for their first income.

faqs:
  - q: What topics does the training cover?
    a: >-
      We choose modules for your group from budgeting, managing debt and mobile loans, saving
      and reserves, SACCO membership, insurance, retirement planning, ethical and
      Shariah-compliant finance, and money in the family. Every session uses Kenyan examples and
      leaves people with one or two habits to start straight away.
  - q: How long are the sessions, and how many people can attend?
    a: >-
      Sessions usually run from two hours to a full day. A single workshop can introduce the
      basics; a series of shorter sessions changes habits more reliably. We recommend groups of
      up to thirty for discussion-led work, and can run larger events with breakout groups if
      you need them.
  - q: Do you deliver training in Kiswahili?
    a: >-
      Yes. Sessions can be run in English, in Kiswahili, or in a mix of both, depending on what
      your group is most comfortable with. Materials are prepared in the same language. Tell us
      about your group during the first call and we will plan the language and examples to suit
      them.
  - q: How do you measure whether the training worked?
    a: >-
      Every programme includes a short assessment before and after, covering knowledge,
      confidence and a few practical habits. We report the results plainly, including what did
      not change. For longer programmes, we can follow up a few months later to see which habits
      have lasted.
  - q: Will you promote products to our staff or members?
    a: >-
      No. Our training is education, not sales. We do not recommend specific financial products,
      and we receive nothing from any provider for what we teach. Participants learn how to ask
      the right questions and compare options for themselves, which is what makes the training
      useful long after we leave.
