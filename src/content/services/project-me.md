faqs:
  - q: What is the difference between monitoring and evaluation?
    a: >-
      Monitoring is the routine tracking of activities and results while a programme runs: how
      many people trained, how many loans repaid. Evaluation steps back at set points to judge
      whether the programme achieved its goals, why, and at what cost. Good M&E designs both
      together, so one feeds the other.
  - q: Can you work to our donor's reporting requirements?
    a: >-
      Yes. Most funders have their own frameworks, indicators and templates. We start from those
      requirements, map them to your programme, and design tools that collect what the funder
      needs without burdening your team. Reports follow the funder's format, with plain-language
      summaries for your board.
  - q: Do you specialise in any kind of programme?
    a: >-
      We work across sectors, with particular depth in financial-inclusion, savings-group,
      livelihoods and financial-literacy programmes, where our consultancy background helps us
      ask the right questions. For specialist technical areas, such as health outcomes, we work
      alongside subject experts and say so in the proposal.
  - q: How do you collect data in the field?
    a: >-
      We use a mix of surveys, interviews, focus groups and programme records, chosen for the
      questions being asked. Tools are tested before use, enumerators are trained, and data is
      checked as it arrives. Participants give informed consent, and personal data is handled
      under the Data Protection Act 2019.
  - q: What if the evaluation shows the programme did not work?
    a: >-
      Then the report says so, clearly and with the evidence. An evaluation that only finds
      success is not worth paying for. We explain what did not work and why, what did, and what
      could change. Funders generally respect an honest account far more than a report that
      reads like marketing.
